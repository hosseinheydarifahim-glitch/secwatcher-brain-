# Authority Strategy

> SecWatcher's goal is not to become the loudest voice in cybersecurity. Its goal is to become the most trusted source of intelligence on fintech security in Iran.

---

# Purpose

Authority is a strategic asset.

It cannot be purchased.

It cannot be manufactured through marketing.

It is earned through consistently demonstrating original understanding.

Every product, research project, public report, and technical article should strengthen SecWatcher's authority.

Authority is not a marketing objective.

It is a business strategy.

---

# Definition of Authority

For SecWatcher, authority means:

> When security professionals need reliable insight into fintech security in Iran, SecWatcher is the first source they trust.

Authority is measured by trust, not popularity.

# Authority Pillars

Authority is built from five pillars.

---

## 1. Original Research

The strongest form of authority.

Publish findings that originate from SecWatcher.

Examples:

- Threat reports.
- Fraud trend analysis.
- Campaign investigations.
- Detection experiments.
- Ecosystem observations.

Never publish research simply because others already have.

---

## 2. Product Intelligence

Products generate knowledge.

That knowledge becomes content.

Example:

```
Detection

↓

Analysis

↓

Research

↓

Public Insight
```

Products should continuously create publishable intelligence.

---

## 3. Technical Education

Teach concepts that improve professional understanding.

Examples:

- How phishing infrastructure evolves.
- Risk scoring methodology.
- Detection engineering.
- Security architecture.
- Fraud patterns.

Education should increase competence.

Not create fear.

---

## 4. Transparent Engineering

Explain how products are designed.

Share:

- Design decisions.
- Trade-offs.
- Failures.
- Lessons learned.
- Engineering principles.

Do not expose sensitive implementation details.

Transparency increases credibility.

---

## 5. Ecosystem Understanding

Publish observations that require local knowledge.

Examples:

- Iranian infrastructure trends.
- Payment ecosystem analysis.
- Local attack techniques.
- Regional fraud behavior.
- Regulatory implications.

This knowledge differentiates SecWatcher from global vendors.

# Content Rule

Every publication should answer at least one question.

- What did we discover?
- What did we learn?
- What changed?
- What evidence supports this?
- Why should security professionals care?

If none can be answered, the content should not be published.

---

# Claims Policy

Every technical claim must satisfy one of three conditions.

## Directly Measured

Supported by SecWatcher's own data.

Preferred.

---

## Directly Observed

Observed during research or investigations.

Acceptable.

---

## Clearly Identified Opinion

Presented as reasoning rather than fact.

Acceptable when explicitly identified.

---

Claims should never depend on marketing language.

# Authority Test

Before publishing anything, ask:

- Is this based on evidence?
- Is this within our intellectual territory?
- Does this increase trust?
- Is it technically defensible?
- Could only SecWatcher publish this?
- Will this remain valuable one year from now?

If most answers are "no", the content does not strengthen authority.
